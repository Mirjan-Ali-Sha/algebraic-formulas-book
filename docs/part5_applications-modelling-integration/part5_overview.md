# Part 5: Applications, Modelling, and Integration

Algebra becomes truly powerful when it steps out of abstract exercises and into real situations: predicting growth, optimising designs, balancing forces, or analysing data. Part 5 is devoted to **applications and modelling**, where you will use the identities, equations, functions, and series developed in earlier parts to describe and solve real-world problems in a structured way. [file:1]

The chapters in this part follow a common pattern. Each begins with a motivating scenario—a story or practical question—followed by a clear statement of the quantities involved and the relationships between them. Visual representations (diagrams, graphs, tables) sit alongside algebraic equations and formulas. You will practise translating between words, pictures, and symbols, building models, refining them when necessary, and interpreting the results thoughtfully. [file:1]

Topics include modelling with linear, quadratic, exponential, and logarithmic functions; using sequences and series to understand savings plans, loan repayments, and repeated processes; and exploring how algebra interacts with geometry, physics, and other domains. You will also work through multi-step problems that combine several concepts—for example, using a quadratic model within an exponential context, or applying inequalities and identities inside a practical optimisation question. By the end of Part 5, algebra should feel less like a separate “subject” and more like a flexible language for thinking about patterns, change, and structure in the world around you. [file:1]
