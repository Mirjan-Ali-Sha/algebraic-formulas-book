# Part 3: Equations and Progressions

Part 3 turns the spotlight on two powerful themes: solving equations that reach beyond the linear case, and understanding sequences and series as algebraic patterns stretched out over time. The key new character is the **quadratic equation**, whose graph is a curve rather than a straight line, and whose solutions can be found by factorization, by completing the square, or by the famous quadratic formula. Alongside quadratics, you will meet arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic progressions, and see how their formulas distil long lists of numbers into compact algebraic statements. [file:1]

The first group of chapters in this part develops quadratics systematically. You will begin with the basic shapes of quadratic functions, the meaning of roots, and the connection between factored forms and graphs. Then you will see how to derive and use the quadratic formula, and how to interpret the number and type of solutions in terms of the discriminant. Throughout, visual reasoning—parabolas opening up or down, areas of rectangles and squares, and balance diagrams—supports the algebraic steps.

The later chapters in Part 3 shift to progressions and series. You will see arithmetic sequences as repeated addition, geometric sequences as repeated multiplication, and harmonic sequences as a more subtle pattern involving reciprocals. From there, you will build formulas for the \(n\)th term and the partial sums, and you will explore both finite and infinite series through visual patterns and algebraic manipulations. By the end of Part 3, equations and progressions should feel like two sides of the same idea: algebra as a language for describing change and accumulation over time. [file:1]
